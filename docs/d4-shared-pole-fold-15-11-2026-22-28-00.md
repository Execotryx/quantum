# D4 shared pole fold and stage profile

Both profiled control builds matched their frozen complete KMB hashes.
The groups 1–7 control also reproduced the result on all four original
256-shot batches. It corrected three errors and retained the one genuine
second-head pole error. The new report includes the backend identity in
addition to the matching observations.

The shared fold keeps one register layout. On a collapsed chart it
substitutes a second head of 1 and uses the continuation U + qV.
The ordinary folds then return D = U and a numerator off by qD.
A chart-controlled shear corrects it. Both quotient pivots are finite
in either chart. This removes the separate chart-controlled arithmetic
schedule of the first shared prototype. The product of the highest
continuation word and the second head's slope is zero on each supported
chart, so the common denominator layout needs no overflow slot at
this boundary.

The targeted Linux suite passed all 16 tests. The shared chart test
checked output values, coherent chart preservation, inverse, scratch,
and inserted phase fingerprints under two simulator seeds. The reduced
GF(2) check exhausted 72 regular and 48 collapsed cases at the smallest
supported register shape and verified the unique inverse within each
chart. Pyright, Ruff, and strict OpenSpec validation passed.

Native profiles of the shared fold, including its inverse, used 66
extra declared qubits on each supplied layout. It cost 3,564 static
gates at 6 and 4 continuation words, 8,604 at 20 and 18, and 14,004 at
35 and 33. These are standalone resources. In particular, the largest
layout already declares 931 qubits by itself. The full decoder needs
a proven degree schedule, the existing omitted-monic representation,
and a lower total cost before pricing. The profile gives a concrete
bound for this construction, while the faster existing absorb kernels
remain available for integration experiments.
