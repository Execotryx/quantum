# D4 successor early-tail repair trial

The byte-identical Syed v12 builds cleared all six D4 captures and
the representative resource screen. Their targeted failure boundary
was weight 15. The pinned verifier gives weights below 15 about
5.04 × 10⁻⁹ of the nominal sampling probability. The targeted gate
therefore provides evidence about the admitted domain that two
representative panels cannot supply.

The experimental builder replaces the first-three-pivot extraction
with the source package's complete raw-query extraction and activates
its reversible rolling tail at slot zero. It shares the original
GF(2²⁴) basis and node map. It leaves the original v12 files and
artifacts intact. The emitted circuit has 889 declared qubits and
391,639 static Toffoli-like operations, 7,636 above the original.

At both simulator seeds and with random and zero masks, the linked
ablation passed the targeted weights 6–35 and all four mixed-pole
fixtures. Each input at weights 0–5 still changed syndrome bits and
left scratch wire 840 set. This wire holds the original carry-one
register in the key builder. The before/after circuit observation
does not identify the first failing phase or value boundary.

The independent low-weight panel used 24 cases per weight from 0
through 8 and two simulator seeds. All weight-0 through weight-5
cases failed with dirty scratch. Every weight-6 through weight-8
case passed. This confirms the separated behavior on those supports.

An extraction-only round trip and the complete projected key
round trip both returned all inputs and scratch and repaid phase
on the same low-weight fixtures. Adding the outer conversion and
projective finish with their shared return reproduced the residual
failures even with the residual scan absent.

The live denominator still has degree 35 with a nonzero leading
coefficient and coprime even/odd square-root halves. The scalar
half-Euclidean replay found degree drops of 13 at weights 0–1, 9 at
weights 2–3 and 5 at weights 4–5. The passing weight-6 through
weight-15 controls had only unit drops. The inherited converter
admits drops of at most two, with additional exclusions for paired
gaps. These captured polynomials explain why extending the tail
alone leaves the low-weight failures.

A scalar ablation of the projective coordinate map
`D'(Y) = Y^35 D(a + 1/Y)` tested origins 0–31. Each origin 1–31
placed all 16 captured denominators within the converter's tested
degree-gap domain. This is a change of coordinates and requires a
matching map for the numerator and scan residues. The leading
coefficient becomes `D(a)`, so a production design also needs a
reversible path for a root at the chosen origin. The scalar check
does not establish those conditions or a native pass.
