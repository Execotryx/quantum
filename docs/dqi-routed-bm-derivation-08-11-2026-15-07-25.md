# Routed BM derivation and bounded evidence

This implementation follows the rescaling and signed-counter branch policy in [Gu et al., SI I.1, Shared BM updates and Reconstructing BM history](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22367v1), using this repository's `S[0] = S_1` convention. The state is `(sigma, tau, d)` with initial `(1, 1, 0)`; the implicit old discrepancy is `delta = 1/sigma_0`. For round `r`, form `T=z*tau`, `Delta=sum_i sigma_i*S[r-1-i]`, and, if nonzero, `alpha=Delta*sigma_0=Delta/delta`.

For a zero discrepancy the round keeps `sigma`, sets `tau=T` and increments `d`. On retain (`Delta!=0`, `d<0`), it sets `sigma'=sigma+alpha*T`, `tau'=T`, increments `d`, and saves `Delta`. On replace (`Delta!=0`, `d>=0`), it routes `sigma` and `T` through the same multiply-add with multiplier `alpha^-1`: `sigma'=T+alpha^-1*sigma`, `tau'=sigma`, `d'=-d`. This differs from the unrescaled `sigma+alpha*T` by the nonzero scalar `alpha^-1` in characteristic two. Because `T_0=0`, retain preserves `sigma_0*delta=1`; replace gives `sigma'_0=1/Delta`, so its new implicit `delta` is `Delta` and the invariant persists.

The replace inverse recovers `Delta=1/sigma'_0`, `sigma=tau'`, and `T=sigma'+alpha^-1*tau'`; it then removes the leading zero to recover old `tau` and negates `d'`. The old stored discrepancy is also recoverable as `sigma'_0*(tau'_0)^-1*Delta=1/tau'_0`. A retain inverse uses its saved nonzero `Delta`, recovers `T=tau'`, reverses the multiply-add, removes the shift, and decrements `d'`. The zero branch has discrepancy zero and uses the same inverse shift/counter without a saved field value. A static exact history record can encode replace in one bit and a 12-bit slot containing the retain discrepancy or zero; this costs 13 bits per round, 910 bits for 70 rounds. This is a storage accounting bound, not a reversible circuit or an assertion about a smaller stack's worst case.

For unrestricted inputs, induction gives degree bounds `deg(sigma_r)<=r` and `deg(tau_r)<=r`: each round shifts the preceding template once, and the update combines polynomials within that range. Both bounds are tight over unrestricted prefixes: all-zero syndromes attain `tau=z^r`, and a prefix of `r-1` zeros followed by one nonzero value attains locator degree `r`. The signed counter lies in `[-(r-1),r]`; both endpoints are reachable, so eight signed bits suffice for 70 rounds. The machine-readable receipt lists all 70 bounds and 13r static history bits. The paper's smaller correctable-domain locator bound and its 35-entry stack claim are not extrapolated here into a proved quantum layout. In particular, blindly discarding high template coefficients breaks inversion even on the all-zero path.

Exhaustive GF(16) tests enumerate all 65,536 four-symbol syndromes, check every forward round against the equivalent unrescaled correction and the discrepancy invariant, and reverse every round and complete history. All 23,851 decodable inputs agree with an independently coded normalized BM final locator. Across those decodable inputs, the per-round maxima are sigma degree `[1,2,2,2]`, tau degree `[1,2,3,4]`, and retained nonzero discrepancy count `[0,0,1,1]`. The paper's tie policy can differ from the conventional oracle outside the decoding domain: `[1,0,0,1]` yields degree-three normalized locators `[1,0,1,1]` and `[1,0,0,1]` respectively. One seeded GF(4096) case at each supported weight 0–35 matches the official-indexed locator and round-trips. These sampled full-field tests are not full-domain proof.

A separate three-word native primitive implements only the conditional row routing and shared product-XOR with caller-supplied multiplier and branch. It and its adjoint each use 198 Toffoli-like gates at 88 declared qubits, restore all input registers on composition, and have clean scratch and phase in the native fixture. It does not compute discrepancies, conditional inverses, template shifts, counter or history and is not a complete round. Forward/reverse receipts and classical bounds are in `docs/evidence/dqi-routed-bm-20261108/`. The 39 classical tests, one native fixture, Ruff, Pyright and strict OpenSpec validation passed. Full-size exact locator assembly and admission remain open.
