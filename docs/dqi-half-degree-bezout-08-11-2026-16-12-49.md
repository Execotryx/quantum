# Half-degree Bézout reciprocal development

For a locator `sigma` in characteristic two, take field square roots of its even and odd coefficients to form `sigma=E^2+z*O^2`; then `sigma'=O^2`. The implementation runs an exact classical polynomial Euclidean algorithm on `(E,O)`, records every quotient, normalizes the final constant gcd, and returns `A,B` with `A*E+B*O=1`. Squaring this identity and using `E^2=z*O^2 mod sigma` gives `(z*A^2+B^2)*sigma'=1 mod sigma`, so reduction of that expression is the desired reciprocal. Nonconstant locators with a nonconstant gcd are rejected as non-square-free. A nonzero constant locator is the explicit zero-weight case and yields reciprocal zero.

The classical fixture agrees with the independently implemented full-degree derivative reciprocal for all 1,815 nonzero scalar multiples of the correctable GF(16) locators of degree zero, one or two, and one independently generated rescaled GF(4096) locator for each weight 0–35. It checks the Bézout identity, modular reciprocal identity, exact reversal of the remainder sequence using the saved quotient list, and a dropped-history mutation. These are deterministic local samples for degrees above two, not full GF(4096) coverage.

A separate native slice reuses the existing 54-Toffoli field product to apply one supplied monomial quotient to the remainder, A and B row pairs and then exchange them; its separately emitted adjoint reverses the operation. It assumes every omitted high term is zero and does not test or enforce this precondition. At three words per row and shift one, each direction uses 324 Toffoli-like gates and 231 declared qubits; shifts zero and two are also tested with arbitrary valid inputs, nonzero targets, phase and scratch cleanup. Receipts are in `docs/evidence/dqi-half-reciprocal-20261108/`. The 41 focused WSL tests, Ruff and Pyright passed.

For `deg sigma<=35`, both E and O have degree at most 17. After an optional initial zero-quotient swap when `deg(E)<deg(O)`, a classical Euclidean sequence with constant gcd starts at degree at most 17 and has at most 18 nonzero divisions. The degree differences telescope, so its quotient-digit count is at most `17+18 = 35`; a coarse adaptive count using three 18-word row updates would be at most `35*3*18*54 = 102,060` field-product Toffolis per direction. That is not a static quantum-circuit bound: coherent quotient selection, varying degrees, support enforcement, saved quotient/branch history, scratch restoration, and fixed-schedule overhead are unpriced. The native slice does not compute a quotient or implement a general EEA, and no full-degree reversible reciprocal or decoder admission is claimed.
